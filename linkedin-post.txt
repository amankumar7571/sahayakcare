Elderly care in India runs on memory and missed calls. A grandmother forgets her BP tablet, nobody knows until the next hospital visit — and by then it's a crisis, not a reminder.

I built SahayakCare for the Catalyst hackathon's problem statement: an Elderly Care & Medication Management Platform. One shared profile for the elderly person, their family and caretakers — so everyone sees the same medicines, the same appointments, the same truth.

What it does: a passcode-protected care profile with medication schedules you can add and edit, adherence tracking with a live 7-day score and day streak, doctor appointments with the next visit always in view, a care circle for family and doctors, a vitals log, and a reminders engine that flags missed doses and appointments coming up — and drafts an email to the primary caretaker in one tap.

What makes it different: it's built for the person actually using it. Large-text mode, English/Hindi toggle, big touch targets, low-stock warnings before the tablets run out. No account, no server, nothing leaves the phone — all data stays in the browser. And it's honest about its limits: email reminders open in your own mail app, because a prototype shouldn't pretend to be a backend.
